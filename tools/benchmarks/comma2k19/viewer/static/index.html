Plans are ego-frame: x is lateral (left of the car plots left, the axis is inverted for that), y is forward, the ego is the square at (0, 0). The first waypoint sits half a second ahead of the car, not at the origin — a paddock trajectory starts at t = waypoint_dt, so at 27 m/s it is ~13 m out; the recorded future starts there too. The panel draws each plan only as far as the metrics beside it were computed over (3 s), even for models that plan further; the road frame draws the whole plan. The lateral axis is stretched — three seconds of driving is ~100 m long and ~20 m wide, and at true scale two plans a metre apart are two pixels apart — so the panel states its own factor and is not a shape to read distances off diagonally. Its window covers this scene's own spread, ground truth and every model, so two scenes are not to the same scale. One prediction every 0.5 s. These runs score ten chosen segments, so they are recorded valid: false and excluded from paddock report — they are a thing to look at, not a score. Deep-dive any moment with paddock render --bench comma2k19 --sample <id>.
