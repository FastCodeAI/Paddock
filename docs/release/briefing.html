1 · Where we are

Paddock inspects, Gallops trains, Jockey drives

All of this sits under Renesas AI Labs. The parts make one loop: measure, explain, train, measure again.

Key points

  • Paddock scores every model through one adapter interface, on the same samples. Earlier evaluations reproduce bit for bit (892/892, 2,379/2,379).
  • Gallops has proven the loop with six training efforts on Drive-JEPA.
  • Jockey is designed (gallops-v0) and waits on the chip's constraints.
  • 71 PRs merged since September; 14 open, none approved. Review is the critical path.

2 · Risk

A score is not a verdict

Each benchmark measures one slice of driving. A high or low score does not settle whether a model is good.

Key points

  • openpilot ships in customers' cars, yet ranks last open loop. Closed loop, it is in the top group.
  • Qwen-Drive predicts the log best, and is fifth when it has to drive.
  • Doing nothing clever (constant velocity) beats most models open loop.
  • Our answer: several benchmarks side by side, paired comparisons, invalid runs marked, the failure behind each score.
Also at risklicences, review capacity, the date, reproducing numbers across sites

3 · What customers ask

Why Paddock, how easy it is, and how to try it

Five rungs, each asking a little more of the customer and showing them more.

Key points

  • Why: every model reports itself on its own terms. Paddock makes them comparable, trustworthy and explained, and next it reports cost on R-Car.
  • How easy: today, an expert's days. v1.0.0 aims at install to first certified run in under a day.
  • Hands-on: v1.0.0 gives the evidence pack, a guided demo and the self-install kit. v1.1 adds hosted projects for their engineers and AI agents.

4 · Using Paddock

Setup, orchestration, compute, storage, results

Today each step is done by hand. v1.0.0 automates setup and makes results portable and certifiable.

Key points

  • Containers, yes, within limits: one image per model, never data or licensed weights inside.
  • paddock setup by profile, gated by doctor, ending in a certification run.
  • Sizes: S is one 48–96 GB GPU for open loop. M is 2–4 GPUs, adding NAVSIM and one closed loop.
  • Customer IP stays theirs: adapters outside our repo, results in their own location.

5 · What Paddock produces

The failure behind each score, and the cheapest fix

Failure modes, the areas to improve, and the tools that find them. Every change is judged against its baseline.

Found so far

  • openpilot reads its speed 12% slow on KITScenes. Fixed in the adapter: −21.8%, no training.
  • openpilot stalls at standstill: 53% of its lost Bench2Drive points.
  • Drive-JEPA could not see its camera until an adapter fix: 3.57 → 4.13.
  • Drive-JEPA picks about the 15th best of its own 32 plans.

6 · Pathway to Gallops

Fine-tuned results, and how far each holds

Gallops trains against the gaps Paddock measures, and Paddock scores the result: the trainee never grades itself.

Key points

  • Real gains: the re-ranker, open loop (2.52 → 0.97 m); a retrain on NVIDIA's data, on AlpaSim (0.543 → 0.770).
  • Not yet: a closed-loop safety gain on scenes nothing was chosen on.
  • New or existing model? Fix the input, then the choice, then fine-tune. Train new only when no model can meet the constraints.

7 · What comes next

Jockey: borrow the strengths, build for R-Car

None of the four families is strong everywhere. Jockey is one small student that takes the best of each.

Recommendation

  • Learn from a self-supervised video encoder; stay small and temporal like openpilot; read its own speed; three front cameras; choose on closed loop; integer-friendly from day one.
Ask the inference teams, by 13 Novthe target chip's envelope · supported ops and precision · how many cameras · the size and latency budget · the toolchain · the output contract
